# Fair speaking rubric for any speaking task

Version: 2026-10-20-fair-task-rubric-v1. Four equally weighted criteria, each scored 0-4, with N/A for insufficient evidence or no relevant opportunity. The normal maximum is 16 when all four criteria can be assessed.

State the task, success conditions and allowed support before the test. Select the language appropriate to what the class has learned. A textbook connection is a teaching reference; it does not make every example phrase, word or grammar form compulsory.

| Criterion | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task Achievement | Achieves all material goals with accurate, relevant information and an appropriate outcome. | Achieves the main outcome; a small omission does not prevent success. | Achieves part of the task, but misses or confuses an important requirement. | Makes limited relevant progress; most important requirements remain unmet. | An observable attempt does not achieve a task-relevant outcome. |
| Interaction | Responds appropriately and uses relevant questions, clarification or confirmation when needed; closes naturally. | Maintains a mostly appropriate exchange; resolves an occasional misunderstanding. | Participates partly, but several relevant questions or responses remain unresolved. | Sustains little relevant exchange despite genuine opportunities to respond. | An observable attempt gives no relevant response despite a clear interaction opportunity. |
| Language Range & Control | Uses enough appropriate, familiar language to express the intended meaning; minor errors do not obstruct it. | Uses suitable words and sentence patterns; some errors cause occasional ambiguity. | Limited vocabulary or control repeatedly makes important meanings ambiguous. | Mostly isolated words or unclear sentence patterns convey little intended meaning. | An observable language attempt conveys no usable task-relevant meaning. |
| Communication & Fluency | Expresses a clear, sufficiently connected message that the listener can follow for this task. | Communicates the main message clearly, with occasional missing links or unclear references. | Conveys some of the message, but key parts require interpretation or clarification. | Conveys little of the message clearly, even after a reasonable clarification opportunity. | An observable attempt leaves the task-relevant message unintelligible. |

## N/A and totals

N/A is unscored, not zero. Report the sum and the available maximum together, and identify any unscored criteria. For example, 9/12 across three assessed criteria is different from 9/16 across four. Do not add points for an unscored criterion or compare totals from different task conditions as if they were equivalent. If there is no usable student speech, leave every criterion unscored and arrange another opportunity.

## Fair scoring rules

- Judge the stated task and the opportunities actually provided. Do not add hidden requirements from textbook examples, suggested steps or vocabulary lists.
- Natural equivalents and simple taught language can earn 4. Do not require every target phrase, advanced grammar, long answers or memorized dialogue.
- Allowed maps, expressions, example dialogues, transcript, replay and Help are task conditions. Their use alone cannot lower a score or prevent 4.
- A useful clarification or self-correction can show successful interaction. Do not deduct merely because the learner requests repetition or checks a detail.
- Separate the criteria: minor grammar errors belong in language control only when they affect it; do not automatically deduct the same error from achievement or clarity when communication succeeds.
- Use null (N/A), not 0, when usable evidence or a genuine opportunity is missing. Mark an unobserved goal requirement uncertain; do not blame the learner for AI scaffolding, early closure or technical failures.
- Use 0 only for an observable unsuccessful attempt. Blank recordings, microphone failures and missing transcripts are insufficient evidence, not demonstrated failure.
- Do not infer pronunciation, accent quality, speech speed, pauses, eye contact, confidence, motivation or attitude from transcript text, recording duration or response latency.
- Use criterion-specific transcript evidence and consider speech-recognition uncertainty. Do not reward invented facts; a legitimate offer to check an unknown fact can be successful.
- Score only enabled, relevant criteria. For a presentation with no interaction opportunity, Interaction is N/A; it is not an automatic zero. Keep assessment conditions consistent across learners.

## Teacher procedure

1. Agree on what a successful response would accomplish, using the task's facts and taught language. Keep permitted support and the speaking opportunity consistent.
2. Calibrate with two or three sample performances. Discuss differences in criterion scores before marking the class.
3. Record a short quotation or observable action for each score. Check uncertain transcription against the recording when available.
4. Give one specific strength and one achievable next step. A suggestion to improve a sentence is not an automatic score deduction.
5. Use teacher judgment and evidence from more than one activity for consequential school reporting. These local task scores are not official Japanese report-card grades or CEFR levels. Learning attitude is not inferred from one recording.

## Calibration examples

- A student asks for repetition, then gives an appropriate answer: the clarification can support a 4 for Interaction.
- A clear short answer or repeated familiar sentence pattern can achieve the task fully; advanced grammar and longer speech are not required.
- A missing article in an understandable order does not automatically reduce Task Achievement. Judge Language Range & Control from the whole available performance.
- If the partner ends the exchange before a required follow-up is possible, record the requirement as uncertain and seek another opportunity rather than treating it as a student failure.

## Design references

This is an original classroom rubric. It uses task-focused, observable communication as its organizing principle, with reference to [NIER's junior-high foreign-language assessment guidance](https://www.nier.go.jp/kaihatsu/pdf/hyouka/r020326_mid_gaikokg.pdf) and the [Council of Europe's CEFR descriptor framework](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors). The four categories and 0-4 scale are local design choices, not an official equivalence table.
