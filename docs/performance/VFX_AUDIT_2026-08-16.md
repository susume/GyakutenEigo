# QuizStrike VFX audit — 2026-08-16

## Outcome

The VFX pass adds a semantic, pooled runtime without adding a particle
dependency. The shipped external art is a seven-sprite subset of Kenney's
Particle Pack 1.1, licensed CC0. The selected runtime folder is 384,727 bytes
including its preserved license file.

Brackeys' VFX Bundle and Kronbits' 1000 Free Game Assets pack were checked as
CC0 alternatives but were not copied into the app because the Kenney subset
covers the current QuizStrike vocabulary with a smaller payload. Per-file
provenance is recorded in `apps/web/public/assets/vfx/LICENSES.md`.

## Runtime budget

| Quality | Active effects | Sprite capacity | Remote view range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Performance / Low | 6 | 6 | 120 world units |
| Balanced / Medium | 12 | 24 | 200 world units |
| High | 16 | 48 | 280 world units |

Local feedback bypasses distance culling and receives admission priority.
Major objective and round cues can replace lower-priority ambient cues. Shared
geometry and fixed slot/material pools avoid per-event geometry allocation;
effects are recycled and fully disposed with the arena scene.

## Follow-up audit corrections

A second implementation audit fixed issues found after the initial pass:

- Hit confirmation now uses a short-lived independent marker. It no longer
  stays yellow after the first hit or replays when a later shot misses.
- Sprite rotation is applied through `SpriteMaterial.rotation`, which is the
  rotation consumed by Three.js's billboard shader.
- Pool admission searches all free slots before evicting an active effect,
  chooses the lowest-priority, oldest eligible effect when full, and never
  lets lower-priority ambience truncate a major cue.
- Reported sprite budgets now match the actual fixed capacities: 6/24/48.
- Snow, water, metal, stone, sand, dirt, and grass cues receive appropriate
  texture routing and neutral surface colors instead of defaulting to team
  blue.
- Heavy fire uses one correctly styled pooled world cue, and remote observers
  receive reduced-intensity snow/player/shield impacts without duplicating the
  attacker or target's local feedback.
- Local objective interactions and Blue/Red round results receive local
  admission priority and explicit victory/defeat treatment.

## Visual-quality correction

A gameplay-scale browser review found that the first implementation was not
visually acceptable even though its budgets and lifecycle were correct. It
used a shared expanding wireframe sphere, torus, octahedron, and uniformly
drifting billboards for almost every cue. When a local event spawned near the
camera, the sphere covered the screen with cage-like lines; short combat cues
were also easy to miss at the project's 2.45x character scale.

The corrected runtime:

- removes all sphere, octahedron, and wireframe VFX geometry;
- uses separate motion profiles for combat, snow, rewards, purchases, spawn,
  elimination, objectives, healing, and round celebrations;
- authors particle height and size against the actual character silhouette;
- keeps major-event rings on the ground and uses only a narrow translucent
  objective beam;
- replaces the lightning-cloud and crosshair-like source selections with a
  crisp hit glint, reward sparkle, projectile flare, and snow-launcher plume
  from the same official CC0 Kenney pack;
- previews FPS cues in front of the player rather than at the camera position;
  and
- enforces the same 6/24/48 global visible-sprite budgets after the richer
  per-effect authoring.

The Character Lab debug panel was reviewed in both overview and Playable FPS
states for weapon fire, wall/player/snow hits, correct-answer rewards,
purchases, elimination, spawn, flag capture, and round victory.

## Positioning correction

A subsequent first-person review found that event coordinates still mixed
player eye height, map ground height, collision points, and authored local
particle offsets. Worse, the presence of any `playerId` moved every effect to
the weapon muzzle. The resolver now uses explicit semantic anchors:

- weapon fire and tracers follow the animated muzzle;
- player hits, snow impacts, and shields follow the animated torso;
- spawn, elimination, healing, rewards, objectives, and round cues use the
  character's feet or the objective's terrain-aware base;
- surface impacts preserve the exact world collision point; and
- hit confirmation, incoming damage, zoom, and cooldown remain HUD feedback
  instead of spawning world effects inside the local camera.

Particle profiles now start at their resolved anchor rather than adding a
second hard-coded body-height offset. The FPS Character Lab preview places
target cues downrange on the aim line, where a target would actually be, rather
than beside the weapon and over the movement joystick.

Weapon cadence was tightened after motion testing: standard muzzle fire now
clears in 90 ms, tracers in 100 ms, and heavy muzzle fire in 115 ms. Character
Lab uses these real timings rather than slowing preview effects, preventing a
brief combat impulse from reading as a lingering world marker.

All three weapon IDs are now explicit in the VFX path: Starter uses the cold
standard muzzle cue, Quick uses a shorter amber rapid-fire cue, and Heavy uses
the broader heavy muzzle cue. Local first-person fire resolves from the actual
weapon muzzle, while bot and remote fire resolves from the animated remote
muzzle. This path is shared by Desert Citadel, Iron Junction, and Temple
Runoff, including their elevated floor positions.
